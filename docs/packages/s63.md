# s63: a 59.94 fps clip keeps its last frame (schrödinger, sonnet, branch `wp/schroedinger-cut-frame-times`)
**Why:** `c26` (report `a51b9e8`, "Needs a decision 3"; pose-spike.md in docs/, the `lateral-raise-cable-2` paragraph
at about line 229) found that `example/lateral-raise-cable-2` (1080×1920, 60000/1001 fps, 180 frames, timescale
1/60000) lost its last frame in both shells: M's range ends on frame 179 (t1 = 2.98632 s), but the app's track stops
at 178. nordmu, 2026-11-04: fix it, with a regression test on a 59.94 fps clip.
The likely cause, reproduced on the laptop (2026-11-04, phase-0 `cd9fbd2`, Node with `fake-webcodecs.js`):
`cutVideo()` (app/src/media/cut.mjs) writes the cut through mediabunny's MP4 muxer, which stores video at a fixed
timescale of 57600 when no frame rate is given (`GLOBAL_TIMESCALE`, about line 31494 of the vendored build in
app/vendor/mediabunny/; the timescale is picked at about line 31706). 1001/60000 s is 960.96 ticks, so every frame
time in the cut is rounded to a whole tick, by up to ±8.7 µs. The range `cutVideo()` returns is computed from the
**source's** times (`range.t1 - start`, cut.mjs about line 100), not the cut's. Frame n (counted from the cut's first frame) rounds up when
(n × 0.04 mod 1) ≤ 0.5: cable-2's frame 179 lands at 2.9863194 s against t1 2.9863167 s, 2.78 µs late, past the
1 µs slack of `framesInRange()` (app/src/analysis/job.mjs, `SLACK_S`) and the sample table's `indexRange()`
(app/src/media/sample-table.mjs, `SLACK`), so the job poses 179 of 180. The cut's own rate reads 59.93999 fps
(c26's number). The start loses a frame the same way when the first frame's time rounds down: a range from frame 13
(rounded 8.3 µs early) loses frame 13. A 30, 60 or 25 fps clip has exact ticks at 57600 and loses nothing; phone
recordings with a 90000 timescale and a variable rate would round too. Measured on phase-0:
| clip and range | the source's frames in the range | the cut's frames in `cut.range` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cable-2, [0, frame 179] | 180 | 179 |
| an 80-frame 59.94 fps testsrc2 clip (step 1's), [0, frame 79] | 80 | 79 |
| the same, [frame 13, frame 40] | 28 | 27 |
| the same, [frame 61, frame 79] | 19 | 19 |
That the phone lost cable-2's frame this way is likely, not proven: step 2 confirms it on cable-2 if the file is there.

3. Tests first, in tests/app/cut.test.js, on the new clip, each failing on phase-0:
   - a cut of the whole clip: every frame of the range is in the cut's range: `framesInRange()` of the cut's
     `frameTimes` over `cut.range` is 80, and the frame source's `frames(cut.range.t0, cut.range.t1)` on the fake
     decoder (`createFakeWebCodecs()`) yields 80 frames;
   - a cut of [frame 13, frame 40]: 28 frames, the first and the last included;
   - for both: `cut.range.t0` and `cut.range.t1` lie within half a frame (1001/120000 s) of the cut's first and last
     frame of the range (here the marks sit on frames), and each of the cut's frames is within half a frame of its
     source time minus `cut.start`;
   - `keepVideo()`'s stored lift carries the same range (the job reads it from there).
   The 30 fps clips' existing tests keep their expectations (their ticks are exact at 57600).
4. The fix, in cut.mjs: the range `cutVideo()` returns never leaves a frame of the range outside it in the cut's own
   times. Read the cut's packet times back after `output.finalize()` (with mediabunny, `metadataOnly`, as the function
   reads the source's) and widen the range only as far as the rounding moved the range's first and last frame (`r.first`,
   `r.last`): `t0` at most the cut's time of the first, `t1` at least the cut's time of the last, otherwise as today
   (`range.t0 - start`, `range.t1 - start`, held to the video's span). The existing tests pin a mark between frames and
   past the end ("the cut's duration and where t0 sits inside it": `over` keeps `t1` 1.0 s though the cut's last frame
   is at 0.967 s; "a mark one ulp short of a keyframe" keeps `t0` exactly 0): those stay. Don't pass `frameRate` to
   `addVideoTrack()`: mediabunny then rounds every timestamp to a multiple of 1/frameRate (`roundToDivisor`), which
   moves a variable rate's own times (contract 5). Don't widen the 1 µs slack in job.mjs or sample-table.mjs unless
   step 2 shows the cut isn't the cause; if you do, say why in the report. (A prototype of this, done while writing the
   package and not kept, gave 80 of 80 and 28 of 28, through `framesInRange()` and `frames()` alike.)
5. Lifts stored before this fix keep their old range, and a re-analysis of one would lose the frame again. Say under
   "Needs a decision" how many frames such a lift can lose (at most one at each end) and recommend whether to repair
   stored ranges; don't build it.
6. The app's README, "Keeping the video": the cut's timescale (57600, mediabunny's) and that `cut.range` is widened to the
