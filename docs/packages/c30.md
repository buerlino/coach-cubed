# c30: the camera on the Fairphone: the permission, the real rate, the ring's memory (curie, opus, branch `wp/curie-camera-phone`)
**Why:** `c27` built the capture on the laptop, against headless Brave's fake camera. Its first real-camera check moved
here (nordmu, 2026-11-02: c27 runs on the laptop alone). The APK can't use the camera yet: its manifest asks for
INTERNET only (the app's README, "The Android shell"; `c20`). Step 3.4 measured the camera in Brave and in the spike's
WebView, not the app's capture (docs/ui-design.md, "Feasibility, from Phase 3"): 30 fps at most in the browser on the
Fairphone, half that in a dim room, hardware H.264 with a keyframe a second. The settings' ranges (a buffer of
10–300 s, a replay of 5–60 s; `RANGES` in app/src/store/settings.mjs) stay provisional until the ring's memory is
measured on the phone (nordmu, 2026-11-02). `c26` measured milestone A on the phone the day before: its numbers are the
baseline here. Recording and analysing together, the wake lock against the real screen timeout, and the app in the
background are `c30b`'s, after this one.
**Read:** app/src/media/capture.mjs; app/src/store/settings.mjs; tests/app/android-shell.test.js; app/tools/check-apk.mjs;
app/tools/check-media-apk.mjs; app/tools/build-apk.sh; spike/capture-check/README.md; spike/capture-check/drive.mjs;
spike/pose-bench/phone-sample.sh. Read these yourself: the manifest (app/android/app/src/main/, `AndroidManifest.xml`;
not bundled, it's XML) and `res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml` beside it; the app's README (in app/, about 1,650 lines)
"The Android shell", "Offline: the service worker", "Capture and the rolling buffer", "R1 and R2, the session", and
both "On the phone" sections (the frame source's and the cut's); c26's report and c20's
(`git log --grep "c26"`, `--grep "c20: report"`), for how the phone was driven and what was measured.
**Red first:** yes
1. The phone (docs/pipeline.md, "The phone"): by day only, and nordmu must know the run is happening, since the
   Fairphone is his own phone. Take its lock (`pipeline/devlock.sh acquire 4d42e62f curie-<run> 1800`, renewed for a
   longer run), keep it awake with `pipeline/stay-awake.sh 4d42e62f`, and name the phone in every adb command
   (`adb -s 4d42e62f …`). If the lock is busy or the phone is missing, say so in the report and stop.
2. Tests first (tests/app/android-shell.test.js, failing on phase-0): the manifest asks for
   `android.permission.CAMERA`, and for nothing beyond INTERNET and CAMERA. It declares the camera as a
   `<uses-feature>` with `android:required="false"` (import works without one). `c43`'s `android:allowBackup="false"` and
   `android:dataExtractionRules` stay exactly as they are (the test already checks them). `c20`'s rule stays too: no
   service worker in the APK.
3. The manifest change, the debug APK rebuilt (`app/tools/build-apk.sh`, in its two slices). The permission at run time:
   the WebView asks Android for the camera when the page calls `getUserMedia`. Check that path once by hand with
   nordmu: Android's dialog appears and he allows it. Then use `pm grant` for the automated runs. A denial must reach
   R1's `camera-denied` panel (`d28`): check it once too. In Brave, serve the PWA through
   `adb -s 4d42e62f reverse` and allow Brave's own prompt by hand once per page session (the spike's README:
   `Browser.grantPermissions` is accepted, and Brave prompts anyway).
4. Measure in the APK and in Brave, with the back camera, in portrait and in landscape, in the room's normal light
   (plus one dim run if you can), medians of at least five runs. Drive the app's own capture over CDP, through the
   screens or a debug page like c34's app/debug/media.mjs. A debug page goes into `dist/` only with
   `assemble.mjs --debug`, never into the shipped one.
   - the real frame rate and size the capture reports, against what was asked;
   - the frames' rotation and the snapshot's rotation: does a portrait phone give a portrait video, upright in s9's
     frame source?
   - the encoder: the config it accepted, the encode time per frame, the dropped frames over 60 s, and the keyframe
     spacing;
   - the ring at `bufferS` 10, 60 and 300: its bytes, and the app's memory (VmRSS of the app and its renderer, as
     phone-sample.sh samples it) over the baseline before the camera opened;
   - `snapshot(20)` and `snapshot(60)`: the time, the size and the peak memory; each snapshot decoded end to end on the
     phone, and s32's cut of a 10 s range of it;
   - the wake lock's state in each shell (held or refused).
   Recordings stay on the phone for the run only and are deleted after it. Never commit a recording, a frame or a
   screenshot.
5. Fix what the phone shows is wrong in the capture (app/src/media/), each fix with a test that fails on phase-0. A
   larger problem goes under "Needs a decision", with a recommendation.
6. The numbers go into the app's README, "Capture and the rolling buffer", in an "On the phone" part, each with how it
   was measured. A recommendation for `RANGES.bufferS` and `RANGES.replayS` goes under "Needs a decision": nordmu
   decides, and settings.mjs keeps its values until then.
**Done when:**
- the test of step 2 passes, and fails on phase-0's code (red first);
- the capture recorded in the APK and in Brave on the Fairphone, or the report says where it stopped and why;
- the report shows `dumpsys package` of the installed app with the camera permission granted, and still without
  `ALLOW_BACKUP` among its flags;
- the report has step 4's table, each number with its method, and the recommendation for the two ranges;
- the phone is put back: `pipeline/stay-awake.sh 4d42e62f --restore-real`, the forwards and reverses removed, the app
  uninstalled, the recordings deleted, Brave's camera permission for the page's origin reset;
- `npm test`, `npm --prefix app test`, `npm --prefix app run test:page` and
  `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_*.py'` pass.
