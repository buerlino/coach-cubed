# c30: the camera on the Fairphone: the permission, the real rate, the ring's memory (curie, opus, branch `wp/curie-camera-phone`)
**Why:** `c27` built the capture on the laptop, against headless Brave's fake camera. Its first real-camera check moved
here (nordmu, 2026-11-02: c27 runs on the laptop alone). The APK can't use the camera yet: its manifest asks for
INTERNET only (the app's README, "The Android shell"; `c20`). Step 3.4 measured the camera in Brave and in the spike's
WebView, not the app's capture (docs/ui-design.md, "Feasibility, from Phase 3"): 30 fps at most in the browser on the
Fairphone, half that in a dim room, hardware H.264 with a keyframe a second. The settings' ranges (a buffer of
10–300 s, a replay of 5–60 s, a delay of 5–20 s; `RANGES` in app/src/store/settings.mjs) stay provisional until the
ring's memory is measured on the phone (nordmu, 2026-11-02). c27's 10 Mbit/s (`BITRATE_1080P30`) and Baseline-first
(`CODECS`) are provisional until this run too (nordmu, 2026-11-03, on c27's report). `c26` measured milestone A on the
phone (merged 2026-11-04): its numbers are the baseline here. `c61` (merged 2026-11-04, on the fake camera) made
`open()`/`resume()` one camera call at a time, a Start cut short reject with `stopped`, and a `failed` capture that
holds its ring resume through "Try again"; those paths on the phone are `c30b`'s. Recording and analysing together, the
wake lock against the real screen timeout, and the app in the background are `c30b`'s, after this one.

**Red first:** yes
1. The phone (docs/pipeline.md, "The phone"): by day only, and nordmu must know the run is happening, since the
   Fairphone is his own phone. Take its lock (`pipeline/devlock.sh acquire 4d42e62f curie-<run> 1800`: it waits up to
   30 minutes for the lock, which then has no expiry; take it once, never again while you hold it, and release it at
   the end with `pipeline/devlock.sh release 4d42e62f curie-<run>`), keep it awake with
   `pipeline/stay-awake.sh 4d42e62f`, and name the phone in every adb command (`adb -s 4d42e62f …`). If the lock is
   busy or the phone is missing, say so in the report and stop.
2. Tests first (tests/app/android-shell.test.js, failing on phase-0; this replaces the assertions at about lines
   41–43, "no camera permission before milestone B" and INTERNET only, and the title of that test, "the committed
   Android project: the placeholder ID, no camera permission, no release signing"): the manifest asks for
   `android.permission.CAMERA`, and for nothing beyond INTERNET and CAMERA. It declares the camera as a
   `<uses-feature>` with `android:required="false"` (import works without one). `c43`'s `android:allowBackup="false"` and
   `android:dataExtractionRules` stay exactly as they are (the test already checks them). `c20`'s rule stays too: no
   service worker in the APK. The README's "No camera permission before milestone B (`c30`); only `INTERNET`" (in
   "The Android shell") and R1/R2's "On the phone (`c30`)" paragraph change with it.
3. The manifest change, the debug APK rebuilt (`app/tools/build-apk.sh`, in its two slices). The permission at run time:
   the WebView asks Android for the camera when the page calls `getUserMedia`. Check that path once by hand with
   nordmu: Android's dialog appears and he allows it. Then use `pm grant` for the automated runs. A denial must reach
   R1's `camera-denied` panel (`d28`): check it once too, and that after a `pm grant` the panel's "Try again" opens the
   preview. In Brave, serve the same `dist/` as c26 did (`npm --prefix app run serve -- --port 8791` on the laptop,
   `adb -s 4d42e62f reverse tcp:8791 tcp:8791`, DevTools through `adb -s 4d42e62f forward`) and allow Brave's own
   prompt by hand once per page session (the spike's README: `Browser.grantPermissions` is accepted, and Brave prompts
   anyway).
4. Measure in the APK and in Brave, with the back camera, in portrait and in landscape, in the room's normal light
   (plus one dim run if you can), medians of at least five runs. Drive the app's own capture over CDP, through the
   screens or a debug page like c34's (`media.mjs` in app/debug/). A debug page goes into `dist/` only with
   `assemble.mjs --debug`, never into the shipped one.
   - the real frame rate (`status.fps`), the frame intervals' jitter, and the size the capture reports, against what
     was asked;
   - the frames' rotation and the snapshot's rotation: does a portrait phone give a portrait video, upright in s9's
     frame source?
   - the encoder: the codec it accepted (`CODECS` in capture.mjs tries `avc1.42002A`, then Main and High; read it from
     the first `follow()` chunk's `decoderConfig.codec`), its real bitrate (`bytes` / `heldS`) while the camera films a
     person moving (the fake camera's still picture gave 0.97 of the 10 Mbit/s asked), the encode time per frame (a
     debug page's own timing: the capture doesn't report it), the dropped frames over 60 s, and the keyframe spacing
     (as check-capture.mjs's `keyframeGapsMs`);
   - the ring at `bufferS` 10, 60 and 300: its bytes and its `heldS` (at least `bufferS`, under `bufferS` plus one
     group: c27's report), and the app's memory (RSS plus GPU memory of the shell's processes, as phone-sample.sh
     samples it) over the baseline before the camera opened. The hardware encoder runs in Android's codec service,
     which neither phone-sample.sh nor check-media-apk.mjs counts: say so beside the numbers. Brave's figure is all its
     processes, other tabs too (c26: 1.39–1.44 GB during an analysis; the APK 172 MB after a cold start), so only the
     difference to the baseline counts;
   - `snapshot(20)` and `snapshot(60)`: the time, the size and the peak memory; each snapshot decoded end to end on the
     phone, and s32's cut of a 10 s range of it; and R3 in each shell (d29's report): the time from R2's Replay to the
     first frame playing, the snapshot played through to its end in R3's `<video>`, ½× and a scrub. R3 paused or
     scrubbed uses s38's stepper, whose hardware decoder held 10–14 frames open on the phone (c26, its "Needs a
     decision" 1): it is in R3's peak memory; say how much, don't fix it here;
   - the wake lock's state in each shell (held or refused);
   - optional, if the run installed the PWA (Brave's "Install", as c26): c26 measured Brave in a tab, not the
     standalone window (`am start` of the installed web app opened a tab; its "Needs a decision" 7). If nordmu has the
     time, he taps the "coach" icon on the home screen once; one session there (R1, R2, Replay, R3) over CDP, and the
     report says whether the capture behaves as in the tab (rate, size, wake lock, R3). One run, no medians.
   Recordings stay on the phone for the run only and are deleted after it. Never commit a recording, a frame or a
   screenshot.
5. Fix what the phone shows is wrong in the capture (app/src/media/), each fix with a test that fails on phase-0. A
