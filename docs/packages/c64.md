# c64: the stepper's decoder holds at most a few frames on the phone (curie, opus, branch `wp/curie-stepper-decoder`)
**Why:** `s38f1` bounded the decoded frames the stepper's paused forward decoder keeps open at 2 (`STREAM_QUEUES` in
app/src/media/stepper.mjs: `maxQueued` 1, `maxQueueSize` 1), checked on fakes and in headless Brave's software
decoder. On the Fairphone's hardware decoder (`c2.qti.avc.decoder`, the debug APK, WebView 153) `c26` counted more
(report `e0a81e0`, "Numbers"; `idleDecoded` in app/debug/media.mjs): **14 open at 440 px, opened and after ten steps,
every run, both clips; at 1320 px 10 opened, 6–11 after ten steps; 0 after the source is closed.** A 1080p decoded
frame is about 3 MB (more with a hardware decoder's padding), outside the stepper's budget and `st.bytes`, held for as
long as the user stays on a frame, and a hardware decoder has few output buffers to share with the analysis and R3b.
No stall was seen (0 in 40 jobs and 100 media calls). nordmu, 2026-11-04, on c26's decision 1: find out why the
WebView's decoder runs ahead despite `maxQueued` 1, and bound it, with this count on the phone as the check. The
cause may lie in the platform (output buffers the WebView or MediaCodec holds); then the package says what the app
can do and what it can't, with numbers. The bound (nordmu, 2026-11-04): at most 4 open frames at 440 and 1320 px,
or numbers showing a platform limit. A candidate to test, not a finding: decodeFrames (app/src/media/decode.mjs)
feeds while `decodeQueueSize < maxQueueSize` and fewer than `maxQueued` frames wait. A hardware decoder takes each
chunk at once (`decodeQueueSize` back to 0, `dequeue` fired) and gives its frame some milliseconds later without
more input, so the loop may feed many chunks before the first frame comes out, and while the stream sits paused at a
`yield` their frames land in its queue. The fakes in tests/app/fake-webcodecs.js only output when fed (B-frames held
back), which would be why the headless bound holds.

1. Tests first, on the laptop. A fake decoder mode modelled on the Fairphone's numbers (step 3 checks the model on
   the phone, and the fake follows what it finds): it takes chunks at once into a pipeline of N slots
   (`decodeQueueSize` back to 0, `dequeue` fired) and outputs each frame a few milliseconds later with no further
   input. The existing at-most-2 test, run with it, must fail on `phase-0` (red first). Keep the existing fakes'
   cases passing, and add two that guard the fix: a decoder that holds B-frames back until it is fed more (the fake's
   `reorder`) still gets every frame out, and so does one that fires no `dequeue` event (Chrome before 106, the S8+'s
   101: `c65`'s case and its fallback, which stay). A bound on chunks in flight that a B-frame decoder can't get past
   is a deadlock, not a fix.
2. Where the frames are, on the laptop: count what the stream feeds and gets back over a step and a pause on the new
   fake (chunks fed, frames out, frames in the stream's queue, frames closed), and say whether the behind decodes

   idle. Bring the same breakdown into `idleDecoded` (app/debug/media.mjs) for the phone: per decoder, chunks fed,
   frames output, where the open ones are, and how long after the last chunk each frame came out.
