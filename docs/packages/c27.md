# c27: capture and the rolling buffer (curie, opus, branch `wp/curie-capture-ring`)
**Why:** Milestone B is the gym session (decisions.md, "What 0.1.0 is", 2026-10-31: "The camera records continuously (a
rolling recording)"; D2: a 60 s buffer, a 20 s replay, several lifts per session). Step 3.4 showed that it works in the
browser: WebCodecs H.264 with a keyframe a second, muxed and cut by mediabunny (spike/capture-check/; docs/'s
ui-design.md, "Feasibility, from Phase 3"). The app has none of it yet. app/src/media/ decodes and cuts files (s9, s32).
S1 shows the gym settings disabled, and R1–R3b (`d28`, `d29`, `d29b`) need a capture to draw. **This package runs on
the laptop alone** (nordmu, 2026-11-02); the first real-camera check is `c30`'s, on the Fairphone. The laptop's
headless Brave has a fake camera that is good enough for this package. coach-a1 probed it on 2026-11-02 through
app/tools/browser.mjs, with `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream=fps=30 --use-fake-ui-for-media-stream` and a page served from
127.0.0.1 (a secure context): `getUserMedia` gave `fake_device_0`, 1920×1080 at 30 fps (20 fps without `fps=30`, though
30 was asked for). It also has `MediaStreamTrackProcessor`, `navigator.wakeLock`, and `VideoEncoder` with
avc1.42002A, 4D002A and 64002A. 90 frames, encoded with a keyframe every 30, gave 90 chunks and 3 keyframes, with the
decoder config on the first chunk.

**Red first:** yes
1. Tests first (tests/app/capture.test.js, in Node, on fakes; they fail on phase-0, where none of it exists):
   - the real frame rate comes from the frames' timestamps (the median interval over the last 2 s), never from the
     track's settings: a fake camera that asks for 30 fps and delivers 20 reports 20;
   - a keyframe is forced by time, at the first frame 1 s or more after the last keyframe, never every N frames; this
     holds when the rate drops from 30 to 15 in the middle of a recording;
   - frames are dropped and counted while the encoder's queue is backed up (the spike drops above 4), never queued
     without bound;
   - the ring holds at least `bufferS` behind its newest frame and drops whole groups of pictures from the front,
     never part of one; its held seconds and bytes are in the state;
   - `snapshot(seconds)` gives an MP4 that starts on the keyframe at or before the newest frame's time minus `seconds`.
     Its first packet carries the decoder config, and its times start at 0 on that keyframe. s9's `openFrameSource`
     opens it and returns every frame in order. s32's `cutVideo` cuts a range from it, which `openFrameSource` opens
     too. A snapshot never spans a change of decoder config: after the camera came back at another size, it starts
     at the first keyframe after the change;
   - `follow(t)` yields the ring's chunks from the keyframe at or before `t`, then each new chunk as it is encoded. It
     ends at `stop()`, or when its caller stops reading. If the ring drops chunks before the follower read them, it goes
     on from the oldest keyframe left;
   - errors are `CaptureError`s with a code: `insecure` (no `navigator.mediaDevices`), `no-camera`, `camera-denied`
     (`NotAllowedError`), `camera-busy` (`NotReadableError`), `no-encoder` (no `VideoEncoder`, or no H.264 config it
     supports), `encoder-failed`; each from the browser's own error, with its message kept;
   - the wake lock: asked at `start()`, asked again when the page becomes visible (the browser drops it when hidden),
     released at `stop()`. A refusal is a state (`wakeLock: 'refused'`), never an error;
   - the camera track's `ended` or `mute` puts the capture in `interrupted`: the encoder is flushed and the ring is
     kept. `resume()` opens the camera again and records on into the same ring from a new keyframe;
   - `stop()` stops the encoder, drops the ring and the wake lock; `close()` releases the camera (every track stopped).
   The fakes go in tests/app/fake-webcodecs.js, which tests/'s other media tests use. A fake camera gives frames with
   timestamps at a chosen real rate, and can be ended or muted. A fake `VideoEncoder` outputs the packets of a real
   clip from tests/app/clips/ (read with mediabunny, with its decoder config), one per frame it is given, so the ring's
   snapshots are real MP4s for `openFrameSource` (with the fake decoder) and `cutVideo`.
2. The capture in app/src/media/ (split into modules as you see fit; one public entry):
   ```js
   import { createCapture } from './media/capture.mjs';
   const cap = createCapture({ camera: 'back', quality: '1080p30', bufferS: 60 });  // the settings' fields
   await cap.open();              // R1: the camera for the preview; cap.stream is its MediaStream; nothing recorded
   await cap.start();             // R1's Start: frames -> H.264, a keyframe a second, into the ring; the wake lock
   const off = cap.subscribe(s => …);  // now and at each change: { state, fps, width, height, rotation, sinceS,
                                       //   heldS, bytes, dropped, wakeLock, error }
   const snap = await cap.snapshot(20);   // R3: { blob, duration, start }, the last 20 s as an MP4
   for await (const c of cap.follow(t)) …  // R3b (d29b): the chunks from the keyframe at or before t, then live
   await cap.resume(); await cap.stop(); cap.close();
   ```
   The states are `closed`, `preview`, `recording`, `interrupted` and `failed`. `sinceS` is the time since `start()`
   (R2's clock). The browser's parts go behind the injectable object in app/src/media/webcodecs.mjs (or one like it),
   so tests replace them. Nothing else in app/src may touch the camera or the encoder.
3. The encoding: H.264 for the `quality` setting (`1080p30` is the only value; read width, height and rate from it),
   `hardwareAcceleration: 'no-preference'`, `latencyMode: 'realtime'`, `avc: { format: 'avc' }`. Choose the bitrate
   and justify it in the report. The spike used 12 Mbit/s; c34's phone-like clips are about 10 Mbit/s. The frames'
   `rotation`, where the browser sets it on a `VideoFrame`, goes into the MP4 track's rotation the way s32 keeps a
   file's; it is 0 on the laptop, and c30 sees what the phone does. The `camera` setting picks `facingMode`
   (`environment` or `user`).
4. The MP4 writing: s32's cut.mjs already writes packets with mediabunny. Share its writer where you can without
   changing what `cutVideo` does (its tests stay as they are); otherwise the ring writes its own. No re-encode, no
   decoder in the snapshot.
5. `app/tools/check-capture.mjs` (`npm --prefix app run check:capture`): serves app/ as check-media.mjs does, and opens
   headless Brave only through app/tools/browser.mjs, with `--use-fake-ui-for-media-stream` and the fake camera. It runs
   twice: once with `=fps=30`, and once without it, where the capture has to report the fake device's own 20 fps.
   Each run uses the real capture with `bufferS` 5: open, start, 12 s, `snapshot(3)`, `snapshot(5)`, a `follow` read
   for 2 s, stop, close. It checks:
   - the reported rate is within 1 fps of the device's;
   - keyframes are 1 s apart, ±1 frame;
   - the ring holds 5 s or more and less than 6 s;
   - each snapshot opens with `openFrameSource` on the real decoder and gives its frames in order, and its length is
     within one keyframe interval of what was asked;
   - `cutVideo` of a range inside a snapshot opens too;
   - the wake lock's state is held or refused, never an error, and every track has ended after `close()`;
   - there is no console error.
   It prints JSON and exits 1 on a failed check. tests/app/browser.test.js's list of tools gains it. The tool and the
   tests leave no temp folder behind (c48, tests/app/tmp-clean.test.js), and no `org.chromium.Chromium.*` folder once
   c52's browser.mjs is in.
